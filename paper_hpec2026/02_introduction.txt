================================================================================
SECTION 2 — INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

The Fast Fourier Transform is a foundational kernel in scientific computing,
signal processing, and increasingly, machine learning (e.g., FNet [ref],
spectral graph networks [ref], and audio feature extraction). GPU vendors have
long provided mature, unified FFT libraries — most notably NVIDIA cuFFT — that
accept an arbitrary transform length N, choose the best internal algorithm
automatically, and expose a single host API call regardless of whether N is a
power of two, a prime, or a large composite.

The Tenstorrent Wormhole B0 is a dataflow neural processor with 64 programmable
Tensix compute cores, 1.5 MB of L1 SRAM per core, and up to 1 GB of on-chip
DRAM. Its fine-grained, software-controlled memory model offers significant
energy efficiency advantages over GPUs for bandwidth-bound workloads such as
large FFTs. However, prior Tenstorrent FFT implementations [Brown25] are
restricted to fixed, power-of-two 2-D sizes and do not expose a unified API.
Users who need an arbitrary-N 1-D FFT — for example, when processing audio
frames of prime length, radar signals with composite sample counts, or
graph-spectral features over irregularly-sized neighborhoods — must either
reshape their inputs to the nearest supported power of two (incurring
accuracy loss and wasted computation) or fall back to a CPU implementation.

This paper closes that gap. Our contributions are:

  (1) A unified Python API, ttnn.experimental.fft(tensor) → (real, imag),
      that is drop-in compatible with the cuFFT host interface and handles
      any N from 2 to ~16 million.

  (2) A compile-time four-tier router that selects the optimal algorithm
      (Stockham, two-pass, three-pass, or Bluestein chirp-Z) based on N
      and the available device memory, with no user involvement.

  (3) Two new L1-safe streaming primitives — rebank_rm and rebank_rm_merge
      — that decompose large tensor reshape operations into 8 KB circular
      buffers, eliminating the L1 overflow errors that previously prevented
      Bluestein and three-pass from operating at large N.

  (4) A core-grid validation loop that prevents dispatch-core placement for
      non-power-of-2 work-unit counts, eliminating "Illegal kernel placement"
      errors that arise when the Wormhole's reserved dispatch cores are
      inadvertently included in a compute grid.

  (5) Comprehensive benchmark results showing up to 3.62 GFLOPs/s throughput,
      1.59× wall-clock speedup over a 24-core Xeon Platinum server CPU at
      N=1,048,576 fp32, and up to 46.9× energy efficiency improvement at
      N=16,777,216 bfloat16.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section II describes the
Wormhole B0 architecture and the ttnn framework. Section III presents the
design of our FFT library. Section IV evaluates performance and accuracy.
Section V discusses related work, and Section VI concludes.
