================================================================================
SECTION 7 — CONCLUSION
================================================================================

We presented ttnn.experimental.fft, a unified 1-D FFT library for the
Tenstorrent Wormhole B0 chip that accepts any transform length N and both fp32
and bfloat16 data types through a single cuFFT-compatible API. The library
introduces two L1-safe streaming primitives — rebank_rm and rebank_rm_merge —
that reduce intermediate circular-buffer requirements from tens of megabytes to
8 KB, enabling Bluestein and three-pass algorithms at sizes previously
impossible on this hardware. A core-grid validation loop prevents dispatch-core
placement for non-power-of-2 work-unit counts, eliminating a class of runtime
errors in the Wormhole kernel scheduler.

Benchmarks on a single n300 Wormhole B0 card show peak throughput of
3.62 GFLOPs/s (fp32) and 3.44 GFLOPs/s (bf16) at N=1,048,576, with up to
46.9× lower energy consumption than a 24-core Xeon Platinum 8260 server CPU
at N=16,777,216 bf16 — using measured device power of 42 W versus 353 W for
the CPU. The wall-clock crossover point, where Wormhole first exceeds CPU
performance, is N≈131,072 for fp32 and N≈1,048,576 for bf16. All fp32 results
satisfy a 5×10⁻⁴ relative-error tolerance; bfloat16 results satisfy 5×10⁻².

Future work includes: (i) extending coverage to 2-D and batch 1-D FFTs to
directly compare with Brown et al. on equal footing; (ii) precomputing
Bluestein chirp sequences in fp64 on the host to eliminate large-argument
sincos error; (iii) applying a Spiral-style automated kernel generator for
per-N Tensix code to fill the performance gap at small N; and (iv) multi-card
(Ethernet-linked Wormhole mesh) FFT for N beyond the 1 GB DRAM limit of a
single n300 card.
